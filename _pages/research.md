## Understanding host–pathogen interactions

My research focuses on understanding how **bacterial pathogens interact with their hosts and shape immune responses during infection**.

My doctoral research at the **National Institute of Animal Biotechnology (NIAB), Hyderabad** focuses on host–pathogen interactions in leptospirosis, with particular interest in how *Leptospira* infection influences host immune responses.

I combine **experimental infection biology, immunology and multi-omics approaches** to identify host responses associated with infection and investigate their biological significance.

## Current research

### Host responses to *Leptospira* infection

My PhD research investigates how *Leptospira* infection alters host immune responses.

I work with **clinical and experimental infection models** and use **transcriptomics and proteomics** to identify changes in host genes, proteins and biological pathways during infection.

These discoveries are followed by **experimental validation** of selected host factors to investigate their potential biological roles during infection.

### From discovery to mechanism

A major interest of my work is what comes **after an omics experiment**.

Large-scale datasets can reveal many genes, proteins and pathways that change during infection. The more important question is:

**Which of these changes actually matter for the biology of infection?**

I am interested in combining **systems-level discovery with experimental biology** to move from infection-associated molecular patterns toward testable biological mechanisms.

My approach can be broadly summarized as:

**Infection → Molecular response → Candidate factors → Experimental validation → Biological interpretation**

## Beyond leptospirosis

Although my doctoral research is centered on *Leptospira*, my broader scientific interest is **bacterial host–pathogen biology**.

I am interested in understanding how bacterial pathogens interact with host cells, how these interactions influence immune responses, and how the resulting host–pathogen dynamics contribute to **protection, persistence or disease**.

For the next stage of my research career, I hope to build on my experience in bacterial infection, immunology and multi-omics while developing deeper expertise in **mechanistic host–pathogen biology**.

## A question that motivates my research

> **How does an encounter between a pathogen and a host cell translate into an immune response—and which molecular interactions ultimately determine the outcome of infection?**
